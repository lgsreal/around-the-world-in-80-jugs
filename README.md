## Mission

AI is changing how software is written, but it does not replace the engineer. **Around the World in 80 Jugs** exists to help developers become better software engineers in the age of AI by strengthening the fundamentals that never go out of style:

- **Software design** — modeling, abstraction, and code that is easy to change
- **Java** — the language, the platform, and its ecosystem
- **Testing** — confidence, feedback loops, and safe refactoring
- **Architecture** — trade-offs, boundaries, and long-term maintainability
- **Debugging** — understanding systems instead of guessing
- **Requirements** — asking the right questions before writing code
- **Critical thinking** — evaluating answers, including the ones an AI gives you

**AI is a tool.** Engineers with strong fundamentals can guide it, review its output, and use it responsibly and effectively. Without those fundamentals, it is easy to ship code that nobody truly understands.

The project is also a celebration of the **global Java community**. Every stop on the journey is a different JUG, country, and culture — and a chance to share knowledge, learn from local communities, and connect developers across borders.

## The Talk

### How to Become a Better Software Engineer in the Age of AI

**Abstract (ready to copy):**

> AI assistants can generate code in seconds, but writing code was never the hardest part of software engineering. Understanding problems, designing solutions, testing behavior, debugging failures, and making sound architectural decisions still depend on the engineer. In this session, Luiz Real explores how developers can grow into better software engineers in the age of AI by strengthening their fundamentals: software design, Java, testing, architecture, debugging, requirements, and critical thinking. You will learn why AI is a powerful tool rather than a replacement for engineering judgment, how strong fundamentals help you guide and review AI-generated work, and practical habits you can apply right away in your day-to-day work.

**What to expect:**

- **Practical** — concrete habits and examples developers can apply immediately.
- **Community-oriented** — an open conversation with plenty of room for questions and local perspectives.
- **For all levels** — valuable for students and junior developers as well as experienced Java engineers and tech leads.

## For JUG Organizers

Would you like **Around the World in 80 Jugs** to stop at your Java User Group? Luiz would love to visit your community — virtually.

| Item | Details |
|------|---------|
| **Suggested talk** | *How to Become a Better Software Engineer in the Age of AI* |
| **Duration** | Flexible — suggested 45–60 minutes plus Q&A (adjustable to your meetup format) |
| **Format** | Online talk or fireside chat, optionally with a guest co-speaker from the Java community |
| **Streaming** | Your JUG's usual platform (e.g., Zoom, Google Meet, StreamYard, YouTube Live). Please share the link and a quick tech check slot in advance. Recording and publishing are welcome if your JUG allows it. |
| **Time zones** | Sessions can be scheduled across time zones — please propose a few dates/times in your local time zone with the UTC offset. |
| **Language** | English by default; other languages (e.g., Portuguese) can be discussed. |

**How to invite Luiz:**

1. [Open an issue](https://github.com/lgsreal/around-the-world-in-80-jugs/issues/new) titled `JUG invitation: <Your JUG name>`.
2. Include your JUG name, city/country, proposed dates and times (with time zone), expected audience, streaming platform, and organizer contact.
3. Luiz will reply to confirm details and add your JUG to the [journey table](#journey--episodes).

> 👉 **Open an issue or [contact Luiz](#contact) to schedule a session.**

## Contributing / Updating the Repo

**Maintainer workflow after each session:**

1. Update the **progress counter** at the top (badge, `X / 80` text, and progress bar).
2. Add or update the row in the **[Journey / Episodes](#journey--episodes)** table: date, JUG, location, guests, language, recording, slides, and status.
3. Add any new guests to the **[Guests](#guests)** table.
4. Add new recordings, slides, or materials to **[Resources](#resources)**.
5. Close the related invitation or guest issue with a link to the recording.
